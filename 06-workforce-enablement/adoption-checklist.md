# Adoption Checklist

Adoption is engineered, not hoped for. This checklist sequences the non-technical work that determines whether a technically successful pilot becomes an operationally successful deployment.

## Before Pilot

- [ ] Executive sponsor identified, briefed, and visibly committed
- [ ] Cohort selected with mixed tenure â€” not just enthusiasts
- [ ] Frontline champions (1â€“2 per team) recruited and given early access
- [ ] Baseline published to stakeholders ([baseline metrics](../05-measurement/baseline-metrics.md))
- [ ] "What this means for your role" message delivered by managers, not by IT
- [ ] Feedback channel stood up with a named owner and response SLA
- [ ] Training scheduled against pilot phases ([training plan](training-plan.md))

## During Pilot

- [ ] Daily floor support in week one; office hours thereafter
- [ ] Weekly adoption telemetry reviewed (active use, feature use, decline patterns)
- [ ] Feedback triaged weekly; fixes and "we heard you" communicated visibly
- [ ] Champions surfacing friction the telemetry can't see
- [ ] Supervisors coaching with assist signals â€” and watching for rubber-stamping
- [ ] Wins shared in the cohort's own words, not corporate voice

## Before Scale

- [ ] Pilot cohort testimony captured (the next team trusts peers, not slides)
- [ ] Training materials revised from pilot friction points
- [ ] Knowledge gaps found in pilot closed before the next cohort inherits them
- [ ] Scale sequencing published â€” who, when, and what support they get
- [ ] Run-rate ownership assigned: platform, knowledge lifecycle, training refresh

## Warning Signs That Gate Scaling

- Weekly active use declining after week three
- Draft acceptance near 100% (rubber-stamping) or near 0% (distrust)
- Feedback volume falling to zero â€” silence is disengagement, not satisfaction
- Workarounds appearing that bypass the assist layer
